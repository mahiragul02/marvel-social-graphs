Marvel Text Detective

Can we use the words in Marvel Wikipedia pages to discover which character is the strongest textual match?

Research Question

Can textual similarity help us identify Marvel characters from their Wikipedia pages?

Instead of relying only on the Marvel network, this experiment asks whether the language used on character pages can reveal relationships between characters. The game turns this analysis into an interactive text-detection challenge.

How it works

Each round compares the mystery character's Wikipedia page with other Marvel pages using a simple Bag-of-Words representation.

Method

The game uses a deliberately simple text similarity method so that the evidence can be inspected directly.

Bag of Words

Each Marvel page is represented by the frequency of the words appearing in it. Word order is ignored.

Page → word frequencies → vector

Cosine Similarity

Cosine similarity compares the direction of two word-frequency vectors. A larger value indicates greater similarity.

similarity(A,B) = A · B / (||A|| ||B||)

What did we learn?

What surprised us?

Textual similarity does not necessarily mean that two characters are directly connected in the Marvel network. Characters can share vocabulary because they belong to similar stories, teams, locations, events, or fictional settings.

What did we check?

Rather than treating the similarity score as proof, the game exposes shared words and a piece of the underlying Wikipedia text. This makes it possible to inspect why two pages were considered similar.



Limitation

Bag-of-Words ignores word order and deeper meaning. Two pages can therefore appear similar because they repeatedly use the same names or topics, even when their actual relationships are different. A TF-IDF representation or a semantic embedding could provide a stronger comparison in future work.
