Marvel Text Detective

Can the language used on Marvel Wikipedia pages help us identify characters and reveal patterns in the Marvel social network?

Research Question

Can textual similarity help us identify Marvel characters from their Wikipedia pages?

The Marvel network tells us which characters are connected, but the Wikipedia pages contain another type of information: language. This experiment asks whether characters with similar Wikipedia descriptions also tend to be connected in the Marvel network.

To explore this question, I built an interactive Marvel Text Detective game. The game uses Bag-of-Words and cosine similarity to compare character pages and then allows the player to inspect the evidence behind each result.

How it works

Each round compares the mystery character's Wikipedia page with other Marvel pages using a simple Bag-of-Words representation.

1

Read the pages

Marvel Wikipedia pages are converted into cleaned word lists. Common stopwords are removed so that frequent words such as "the" and "and" do not dominate the comparison.

2

Compare the words

Each page becomes a word-frequency vector. Cosine similarity measures how similar two character pages are based on their vocabulary.

3

Inspect the evidence

After each guess, the game shows the similarity score, shared words, network relationship, and a piece of the underlying Wikipedia text.

Method

The analysis uses a deliberately simple text similarity method so that the evidence can be inspected directly.

Bag of Words

Each Marvel page is represented by the frequency of the words appearing in it. Word order is ignored.

Page → word frequencies → vector

Cosine Similarity

Cosine similarity compares the direction of two word-frequency vectors. A larger value indicates greater textual similarity.

similarity(A,B) = A · B / (||A|| ||B||)

    How to read the figure

    The figure compares the distribution of textual similarity for connected and non-connected character pairs. If connected pairs tend to have higher similarity, their Wikipedia pages may share vocabulary because the characters appear in related stories, teams, events, or settings.

    However, textual similarity does not automatically mean that two characters are socially connected. Two characters can have similar pages because they share topics or recurring vocabulary even when no direct network edge exists between them.

    What did we learn?

    The analysis is not only about obtaining a similarity score. The important step is to inspect the underlying text and consider what the score actually represents.

    What surprised us?

    Textual similarity does not necessarily mean that two characters are directly connected in the Marvel network.

    Characters can share vocabulary because they belong to similar stories, teams, locations, events, or fictional settings.

    What did we check?

    Rather than treating the similarity score as proof, the game exposes shared words and a piece of the underlying Wikipedia text.

    This makes it possible to ask: Why were these two pages considered similar?



    Limitation

    Bag-of-Words ignores word order and deeper meaning. Two pages can therefore appear similar because they repeatedly use the same names or topics, even when their actual relationships are different.

    Another limitation is that the figure compares connected pairs with a sample of non-connected pairs. A future analysis could use TF-IDF, semantic embeddings, or the complete set of non-connected pairs to obtain a more detailed comparison.



    Takeaway

    The experiment shows that the Marvel network and the text of Wikipedia pages provide two different views of the same fictional universe.

    The network captures explicit relationships, while textual similarity captures shared language and context. Combining the two helps us investigate whether structural relationships are also reflected in how characters are described.
